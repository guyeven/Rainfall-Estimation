\section*{Summary}

The key distinction across the three case-study patches is the size of the pixel-wise change between the ILDW field and the optimized field
\[
|R_{\mathrm{OPT}}(x,y) - R_{\mathrm{ILDW}}(x,y)|.
\]

For the first two patches, the optimizer starts from ILDW and only makes very small adjustments. As a result, the rainy error maps for ILDW and OPT look almost identical. For the third patch, the optimizer moves much farther away from the ILDW initialization, and that larger change is also accompanied by a clear improvement against ground truth. Consequently, the ILDW and OPT rainy maps look visibly different.

\section*{Interpretation}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{Patch 1:} \texttt{RAD\_OPERA\_HOURLY\_RAINFALL\_ACCUMULATION\_202301280900\_patch000} looks the same because the optimizer changes the field only by a few thousandths to a few hundredths of mm/h for most pixels.
\item \textbf{Patch 2:} \texttt{RAD\_OPERA\_HOURLY\_RAINFALL\_ACCUMULATION\_202301310900\_patch000} follows the same pattern. The optimizer improves the objective slightly, but the actual map remains very close to ILDW.
\item \textbf{Patch 3:} \texttt{RAD\_OPERA\_HOURLY\_RAINFALL\_ACCUMULATION\_202301311600\_patch000} is different because the optimizer makes a much larger field update over a large fraction of the pixels. That is why the rainy maps visibly diverge.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Reading the Table}

The first two cases are visually similar because their median and upper-quantile changes are tiny: even the 95th percentile of $|OPT-ILDW|$ stays below 0.04 mm/h. In contrast, for the third case the median change is already 0.205 mm/h and the 90th percentile exceeds 0.6 mm/h. This means a large portion of the field is being reshaped, which is exactly why the rainy maps look different to the eye.

The MAE ratios tell the same story. In the first two patches, OPT improves rainy MAE only marginally, to about 99.8\% and 99.5\% of the ILDW value\footnote{Equivalently, this is a relative rainy-pixel MAE improvement of about 0.2\% to 0.5\%. Here, $\mathrm{MAE}=\mathrm{mean}(|\mathrm{prediction}-\mathrm{GT}|)$. For \texttt{202301280900\_patch000}, rainy-pixel MAE decreases from 0.80682 to 0.80493, i.e.\ $(0.80682-0.80493)/0.80682 \approx 0.23\%$. For \texttt{202301310900\_patch000}, rainy-pixel MAE decreases from 0.56389 to 0.56113, i.e.\ about 0.49\%.}, while in the third patch OPT reduces rainy MAE to about 90.3\% of the ILDW value, which is a much more substantial gain.
